# Why WorldQuant & My Research Philosophy

**Why WorldQuant.** WorldQuant describes its culture around a few ideas that happen to be the ones I test myself against every day: intellectual horsepower, challenging convention, continuous improvement, and accountability for results. That is a description of how I want to work, not just who I want to work for.

**Intellectual horsepower.** At Razorpay I apply statistical modeling across a $500M+ annual-GMV portfolio. I built success-rate models for Meta and Airbnb that drove a +15% Success Rate lift, deployed Z-score thresholding over high-throughput transaction streams to cut failure-detection latency by 10×, and built Python ETL pipelines that eliminated 40% of manual reporting. Behind each of those numbers is a researcher's process: frame a hypothesis, design a metric, test it, and judge the result honestly.

**Challenging convention.** The clearest expression of this is my personal quant research project. I built a full systematic research pipeline from the ground up — a source-agnostic Binance data layer, feature engineering, gradient-boosted classifiers, walk-forward IS/OOS evaluation, a backtesting engine with transaction costs and drawdowns, live WebSocket forecasting, drift monitoring, and even a from-scratch MiniGPT transformer to understand the machinery of modern ML. I did not inherit a convenient methodology; I questioned and rebuilt each step.

**Continuous improvement.** The defining result of that project was its honest negative. My raw momentum features produced roughly 52% directional accuracy that did not cleanly beat buy-and-hold after costs. Most people would quietly abandon that finding. Instead, it taught me my most important research lesson: a signal earns its risk-adjusted place or it does not, and a proper pipeline exists precisely to tell you the truth about that. That is why I now see neutralization and correlation analysis as the natural next problems, and why I am eager to learn the rigorous standards WorldQuant applies to signals.

**Accountability for results.** Whether in production payments or a personal research repo, I hold myself to the observed outcome — measured, cost-aware, and reproducible. I want to bring that discipline to your simulations, benefit research, and signal discovery.

I am early in my formal markets career but fluent in its foundations — linear algebra, statistics, machine learning, Python, and Unix — and I am prepared to work hard to close the gap. WorldQuant is where I want that respect-for-evidence mindset to find its home.
